    Why this page exists
    
    
    One benchmark "redline" had no tracked changes in it.
    
    
    We ran AI agents on two public legal benchmarks, Crosby's RedlineBench and Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark. In one delivered file, old words were struck through and new ones sat beside them. Inside, Word had recorded 39 runs of strikethrough formatting and no insertions or deletions. Accept All does nothing to a file like that. The grader reads only the text, and gave it 0.90 out of 1.
    
    
    In another, the agent accepted the other side's markup and re-issued its own. All 182 insertions carried one name, and nobody could see any longer what the counterparty had proposed.
    
    
    Both files came from the benchmark's own tooling. The same tasks done with jubarte produced 38 tracked insertions and 39 deletions in the first case, and kept 191 of the counterparty's changes beside 89 of ours in the second.
    
  
    

  
    
    
    What we measured
    
    
    No score difference detected. Almost half the time on one benchmark, an eighth less on the other.
    
    
    Each task was run twice with the same model, brief and grader: once with the benchmark's own Word tools, once with jubarte in their place. We hoped the score would rise. It did not. The cost fell.
    
    
    
      
    
      
    
    
    
    
    RedlineBench: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash in Claude Code with jubarte 0.15.0, 125 paired tasks, one judge model (GLM-5.3-Flash). Harvey: 90 paired redline tasks with jubarte 0.16, same model, Harvey's judge. In neither was a score difference detected. Of six cohorts tested, jubarte lost on one: Nemotron 30B, a small model, scored lower with it. Runs used jubarte 0.14 to 0.17, not 0.19. No frontier model was the agent.
